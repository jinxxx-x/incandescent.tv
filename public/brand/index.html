The Brief

You already have equity. What you don't have yet is a system.

Incandescent has a name that does real work — light, warmth, the filament in the dark — plus a tagline worth keeping in "Stories that glow," a documentary pedigree (Netflix, History, Discovery), and a website that already leans warm and cinematic. What's missing is a defined brand: a logo that isn't a stock bulb, a codified palette and type system, and a voice that travels from a pitch deck to a title card to a social avatar. These three directions each take a clear position on how to do that.

What stays constant

  • →The name & "Stories that glow." The metaphor of incandescence is the strategic core. We don't touch it.
  • →Documentary prestige. Emmy-winning, premium, historical. Never cheap, never loud for its own sake.
  • →The work leads. Whatever the system, the films are the hero. Brand frames them, never competes.

What's in play

  • →The logomark. A real, ownable symbol — not a literal lightbulb.
  • →Color & typography. A defined palette with hex values and a paired type system.
  • →Voice & register. How formal, how warm, how modern.
  • →Where it lives. Title cards, lower-thirds, cards, decks, social.

Rationale
The cinematic frame, drawn as a colophon.

The mark borrows from two archives at once: the registration corners of a film frame and the crop marks of a printed page. A high-contrast Bodoni "I" sits inside them like a dropped capital in a first edition. On bone-white paper with oxblood and ink, the company reads less like a startup and more like an institution — exactly right for historical subjects (Sitting Bull, the Revolution, the Titans). Archivo keeps captions and credits clean and contemporary.

Where we'd land

Three honest options — and a recommendation.

DirectionFeels likeStrongest forTrade-off
Ember A prestige end-title Buyers, networks, the deck. Protects existing equity. Safe — it evolves rather than surprises.
Archive A first-edition history Authority on historical subjects; print & credits. Light/editorial can read quiet on screen.
Signal A streamer ident Social, thumbnails, audience-facing, motion. Boldest — furthest from the current site.
Recommendation

Lead with Ember as the house brand — and borrow Signal's ring for everything that lives on a screen.

Ember is the lowest-risk path to a real system: it turns equity you already have into something documented and ownable, and the founders' buyers will read it instantly as premium. But Ember's quiet glow is built for the dark — it can disappear at thumbnail scale. So we'd pair it: Ember sets the master identity (wordmark, palette, decks, title cards), while Signal's radiating mark becomes the motion/social variant — the avatar, the app tile, the animated sting. One warm, literary house; one sharp, broadcast-ready signal flare for the feed. Archive then lives on as an optional sub-treatment for the most historical, print-heavy projects.
